07

Four folders, one direction.

The arrow only points one way. That is what stops the library becoming a second opinion of itself.

What you read
Books, Papers, Clippings. The researcher reads here and never writes here. PDFs stay out of git.
Knowledge/
What it read
One note per chapter read, and a wiki page per idea. INDEX.md is read first, always; LOG.md is append-only.
Studies/
What you worked out
An idea, a plan or a decision, from your library. Strategy work lands in the strategy itself.
Philosophy/
Your voice — the side channel
How you invest, in your own words. Cited by everything, generated from by nothing, and edited only through a diff you approve. Anything you would not want read does not go in the repository at all.
Directionality
Sources/ → Knowledge/ → Studies/

The non-negotiables.

Four lines from a sample RESEARCHER.md. Write your own version of these before you write anything else.
01
Every number about a strategy comes from the Lab's libraries. The researcher never computes one itself.
02
A hypothesis is written before its test, and every prediction in it cites a source. No source, no prediction.
03
A strategy graduates only against criteria written down beforehand. Never on a good month.
04
The design of an experiment is challenged before it runs, not after it fails. Find the flaw while it is still cheap.
How it cites
A claim with no source is written as a lead, never as a fact.

The convention worth keeping
16

The prefix is the address.

It tells you which stage owns a column — and therefore which file to open when it is wrong.
m_*
The provider, via the Curator
Raw market data — what arrived.
Change it: never.
c_*
Curator custom calculations
One security's own history. Nothing else is visible.
Change it: you need a new per-security quantity.
r_*
Refinery custom calculations
Securities against each other, per date.
Change it: you need a rank, a breadth reading, or a fitted model.
current_*
Joined from the security master
Today's classification — not point-in-time.
Change it: you group or report by something new. Never select on it.
The trap
Selecting on current_* is survivorship bias with a respectable-looking column name. It knows today's sector for a company that was something else in 2014.

Module 01 · Data Curator
18

One interface, every provider.

Open source, on PyPI, no account needed. It turns provider chaos into one homogenised time series per identifier.
Configured in the repo
No spreadsheet. Identifiers come from Investable_Universe.csv, dates and columns from the strategy's own parameters — plain files, versioned with the strategy.
Unified access
One interface across data providers and your own in-house files, with the tags homogenised.
Prebuilt calculations
Returns, ratios, trend, volatility, volume — and outlier adjustments — ready to switch on.
Your own c_* functions
Custom calculations extend it. This is where your per-security feature inputs are written.

Data integrity · the short version
21

Trust, then build.

Six checks on what the Curator wrote, before any feature is built on it. Each one is a rule applied to every security and every date — never a judgement call on one row.
01
Splits and dividends
Adjusted prices, or one unadjusted split invents a −50% day and a momentum signal that never existed.
02
The dead are present
Delisted names in the universe with their last price. A universe of survivors backtests beautifully and trades badly.
03
The availability date
Fundamentals carry three dates. The one you may use is when the market could have read it — not the period end.
04
Outliers, by rule
Statistical: is it unusual (z-score, MAD)? Financial: is it possible (negative price, volume of zero for a year)? Both, then a rule.
05
Gaps and stale prices
A price that does not move for twenty days is not a calm stock, it is a missing feed. Coverage first, then signal.
06
Every correction logged
Whatever you change lands in Data_Issues.csv with the rule that changed it. A cleaning step you cannot explain is a result you cannot defend.
Section 04

What to look for in the example

the menu of signals · the six ways a number lies to you
22
The menu
23

Pick one, not eight.

Your objective already implies one of these. Build that one properly before you build a second.
Return signals
Earnings surprise — abnormal returns following an unexpected result, and the drift after it.
Short-term reversal — mean reversion after an extreme short-horizon move.
Residual momentum — trend persistence that survives removing factor exposure.
Quality — profitability and balance-sheet strength, priced slowly.
Accruals — mispricing tied to low earnings quality.
Sentiment — predictive content in news, filings and textual tone.
Event-driven — temporary mispricing around M&A, buybacks and guidance.
Risk factors
Market — exposure to the asset class moving at all.
Beta — sensitivity of the security to that benchmark.
Sector — exposure to industry-specific economics.
Size — market capitalisation.
Value — price relative to fundamentals.
Momentum — the persistent trend, as a premium rather than an edge.
Volatility — exposure to return variability.
Growth — exposure to strong fundamental expansion.
Notice
Momentum and quality appear on both sides. Which one yours is depends entirely on whether attribution can explain it away.
Before you believe it
24

Six ways it is a lie.

When a result surprises you on the upside, one of these is usually why. Check them in this order.
Look-ahead
A value indexed on a date before you could have had it. The commonest, and the most flattering.
Survivorship
Your universe is who is still listed. The delisted names are the ones the strategy would have bought.
Selection on current_*
Filtering history by today's classification. Survivorship with a respectable column name.
Undeclared sweeps
Forty lookbacks tried, one reported. The p-value you quote is for the one test you pretended to run.
Uncosted turnover
A signal that rebalances daily on illiquid names is a spread-payment schedule, not a strategy.
Borrowed beta
It works, and it is sector beta. Real, and not a return of your own. Attribution settles this in session 05.
Ask your researcher
/query which of these six does my feature risk, and what did we read about it?
